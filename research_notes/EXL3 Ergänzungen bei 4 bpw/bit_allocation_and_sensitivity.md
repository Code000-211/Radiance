## Key Question 1 — Sensitivity-based allocation methods: how sensitivity is measured, how well layer-local proxies predict end-to-end KL, gains over uniform at ~4 bpw

### Takeaway
Every production pipeline in 2026 (exllamav3 self-calibration, Bartowski's per-tensor layout maps, Thireus' quant_assign, Unsloth Dynamic 3.0, AtomicChat "AD" layouts) has converged on the same recipe: measure a per-tensor scalar sensitivity S_t by perturbing/degrading one tensor at a time and reading the *end-to-end* KL of the final logits (not a layer-local Tr(ΔW H ΔWᵀ) proxy), model cost as additive and ~quadratic in relative weight error, then solve a greedy knapsack. Reported gains over uniform/heuristic allocation at ~4 bpw are 6–54 % lower mean KL at equal size (0.94× for Bartowski Q4_K_M; 0.0158→0.0073 for AtomicChat at 17–19 GB; ">10 %" top-1 for Unsloth v3), and exllamav3's shaped-noise proxy reaches Spearman 0.84 against ground-truth swap attribution.

### Cited Findings

**Classical Hessian-based methods (background)**
- HAWQ-V2: argues "a better sensitivity metric is to compute the average of all of the Hessian eigenvalues" (i.e. Hessian trace, vs HAWQ-V1's top eigenvalue) and introduces "a Pareto frontier based method for selecting the exact bit precision of different layers without any manual selection"; it is a layer-local, second-order proxy (arXiv 1911.03852, 2019) — [HF paper page](https://huggingface.co/papers/1911.03852)
- OWQ: "prioritizes a small subset of structured weights sensitive to quantization, storing them in high-precision" (outlier columns identified via the Hessian diagonal of the layer-wise OPTQ objective) and reports "3.1-bit models using OWQ perform comparably to 4-bit models optimized by OPTQ" (arXiv 2306.02272, 2023) — [HF paper page](https://huggingface.co/papers/2306.02272)
- SqueezeLLM: "sensitivity-based non-uniform quantization, which searches for the optimal bit precision assignment based on second-order information" (Fisher-information weighted k-means) plus "Dense-and-Sparse decomposition that stores outliers and sensitive weight values in an efficient sparse format"; "our 3-bit quantization significantly reduces the perplexity gap from the FP16 baseline by up to 2.1x" (arXiv 2306.07629, 2023) — [HF paper page](https://huggingface.co/papers/2306.07629)

**exllamav3 self-calibration pipeline (doc/optimize.md; author = turboderp)**
- Default `convert.py --bits` uses a "uniform/ends-first allocation ... with promotions at the head and tail of the stack to absorb a fractional budget"; the SC pipeline "measures how much each individual tensor's quantization error contributes to the model's end-to-end output error, then solves for the bit allocation that minimizes the total at a given size budget" — [optimize.md](https://github.com/turboderp-org/exllamav3/blob/master/doc/optimize.md)
- Key rationale quote: "measured on a model's own chat/reasoning output, the familiar U-shape (first/last layers hottest) largely flattens, and small mid-stack attention projections (k/v) become the most valuable bits in the network." Also: "Sensitivities measured on raw text transfer poorly, even if formatted by the model's expected chat template. Sensitivities measured on a self-sampled trace generalize much better" — [optimize.md](https://github.com/turboderp-org/exllamav3/blob/master/doc/optimize.md)
- Stages: `sc_trace.py` (self-sampled trace, default 250×2048 packed rows), `sc_rfn_probe.py` (per-tensor relative error rfn = ‖W_q − W‖/‖W‖ of an existing quant; "typically a ±10% spread at fixed K, with structure by module type"; default global anchor `2:0.292` i.e. rfn≈0.292 at K=2), `sc_measure.py` (noise injection into the *unquantized* model, suffix forward from cached boundary state, KLD of final logits; "a 27B model with two levels takes ~35 minutes"), `sc_optimize.py` (greedy), `convert.py -rcp` — [optimize.md](https://github.com/turboderp-org/exllamav3/blob/master/doc/optimize.md)
- Proxy quality: "Without [--shaped], isotropic noise overestimates sensitivity by up to ~6x on tensors with strongly anisotropic input statistics (v_proj tends to be worst)... Shaped-noise predictions validate at Spearman 0.84 against ground-truth swap attribution of a real conversion (0.61 unshaped), with the residual error nearly uniform across tensors (harmless to the allocation)." — [optimize.md](https://github.com/turboderp-org/exllamav3/blob/master/doc/optimize.md)
- Cost model: `kld(t, K) = S_t * rfn_t(K)^alpha`, "greedy by marginal KLD reduction per stored bit, which is optimal since the curves are convex, plus an exchange-repair pass"; alpha "theoretical value is 2 (quadratic); fitted values ... run slightly low (~1.7–1.9) and real-quant transitions measure ~2.0"; `--alpha_low` "Measured ~2.35 for the K2->K1 octave on Qwen3.8-27B. The law steepens once a tensor is mostly noise." Tied groups default `k_proj+v_proj,gate_proj+up_proj` ("KLD cost tends to be tiny"). It "Prints the predicted KLD against an ends-first baseline at the same budget" — [optimize.md](https://github.com/turboderp-org/exllamav3/blob/master/doc/optimize.md)
- `-hq` flag of convert.py: "Increase the bitrate of select layers, such as attention and shared-expert layers ... for MoE models this is typically a very small increase in size (0.05 - 0.10 bpw) for a disproportionately large increase in model fidelity" — [convert.md](https://github.com/turboderp-org/exllamav3/blob/master/doc/convert.md)
- Caveat from the doc: "The optimization pipeline is experimental and still a work in progress. It is currently untested on sparse models." — [optimize.md](https://github.com/turboderp-org/exllamav3/blob/master/doc/optimize.md)
- Release notes: v1.5.1 (20 Sep 2026) "Self-calibration pipeline improvements (including highly experimental YAQA support)" and "Fractional-trellis quantization mode"; v1.5.4 (3 Oct 2026) "Improved quantization accuracy" and "Options for token-embedding quantization and disk streaming" — [exllamav3 releases](https://github.com/turboderp-org/exllamav3/releases)

### Inferences
- All modern tools treat tensor contributions as additive and use a *degrade-one / perturb-one, read end-to-end KL* measurement; nobody in the 2026 practitioner ecosystem uses a pure layer-local Hessian-trace proxy as the allocation signal. The exllamav3 numbers quantify why: an unshaped (isotropic, layer-local-style) noise proxy only reaches Spearman 0.61 vs ground truth and can mis-rank v_proj by ~6×, while shaping the noise with the quantizer's own LDLQ error distribution raises it to 0.84.
- The gain from allocation is largest at low bitrates (0.78–0.79× KLD at IQ2/Q3) and shrinks to 0.94–0.96× at Q4_K_M/Q6_K in Bartowski's data; AtomicChat's 54 % reduction at ~4.5–5 bpw is larger because their baseline ("every layer treated the same") is a flat layout without llama.cpp's ends-first bumps, and because their eval is in-domain 4K-context text where attention/state tensors matter more.
- For a KL objective on *self-generated agentic traces*, the exllamav3 doc's statement that the U-shape flattens and mid-stack k/v become most valuable is the most directly relevant finding; it implies the calibration/measurement corpus should be the model's own traces, not wikitext.

### Gaps
- No source gives a direct numeric comparison of Tr(ΔW H ΔWᵀ)-predicted vs measured end-to-end KL for a 2025–2026 hybrid model; the closest quantitative proxy-validation is exllamav3's Spearman 0.84/0.61.
- The exact Unsloth v2/v3 selection algorithm (what statistic drives per-tensor type choice) is not published in a form reachable here; only the "KLD-driven, imatrix, no QAT" description.
- The turboderp KLD-vs-VRAM charts for Qwen3.8-27B (PNG on cdn-uploads.huggingface.co) could not be downloaded (403 from this session), so the SC-vs-plain-vs-Unsloth KL numbers at 4 bpw are not reproduced here.

## Key Question 2 — Which tensors are most sensitive in Qwen3-Next / Qwen3.5 / Qwen3.8 / Nemotron-H hybrids

### Takeaway
Three independent measurements on the exact Qwen3.5/3.8-27B architecture (turboderp's SC recipe, Thireus' degrade-one KLD, AtomicChat's ablations) plus Bartowski's Qwen3.5 sweep agree on the ranking per parameter: tiny GDN a/b gates (and conv1d) ≫ attention k/v ≫ lm_head > attn o_proj ≈ GDN out_proj > attn_gate (in_proj_z) > in_proj_qkv ≈ ffn_down ≈ ffn_up > attn_q ≈ ffn_gate > token_embd; depth sensitivity is U-shaped on web text (layer 0 and layers ~51–63, with layer 63 extreme) but turboderp's self-trace-based optimizer *demotes* layer 0 and promotes layers 57–61. The Minima paper (Sep 2026) shows the GDN gates are in fact the *least* sensitive to activation-side error and that the whole GDN block survives NVFP4 W4A4 — so "protect the recurrent half" is a prior, not a measured necessity, although at pure weight-only 4-bit budgets every practitioner still spends slightly more on out_proj and keeps the a/b gates and conv1d unquantized because they cost nothing.

**Minima: "Why Gated DeltaNet Survives 4-Bit Quantization" (arXiv 2609.04098, 3–4 Sep 2026; authors' claims)**
- "Early community 4-bit quantizations of Qwen3.8-27B (48 GDN layers, 16 attention layers) left the GDN block in 8- or 16-bit precision -- especially its decay and write-strength gates -- on the intuition that errors in a recurrence accumulate over long contexts." They quantize "all 496 linear layers" (GDN in_proj_qkv, in_proj_z, in_proj_a, in_proj_b, out_proj; attention q/k/v/o; MLP) to NVFP4 W4A4 (group 16) with calibration-only PTQ on 128 × 32K-token samples; embeddings, lm_head, conv1d, norms, A_log/dt_bias stay BF16 — [HF paper page](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.04098), [minima-ai checkpoint README](https://huggingface.co/minima-ai/mnma_qwen3.8_27b_nvfp4/resolve/main/README.md)
- Mechanism findings: "(ii) the supposedly fragile gate projections are the least sensitive -- softplus/exponential and sigmoid parameterizations compress ~11% GEMM error to ~2% output error; (iii) the delta-rule recurrence holds injected noise at a flat plateau over 32K tokens and forgets a state impulse within hundreds of steps ... (iv) the per-token quantization cost washes out with context instead of compounding" — [HF paper page](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.04098)
- Results (vLLM 0.27.1, one 96 GB Blackwell, vs BF16): WikiText-2 PPL @4K 6.95→7.68 (+0.73), @32K 10.35→10.50 (+0.15); MMLU-Pro 80.4→79.7; GSM8K 95.5→95.5; AIME'25 86.7→86.7; GPQA-Diamond 86.5→85.1; LiveCodeBench v6 79.0→78.5; RULER NIAH 32K/64K 100→100; weights 50.13→17.53 GiB. "No task score is CI-separated from BF16." — [minima-ai README](https://huggingface.co/minima-ai/mnma_qwen3.8_27b_nvfp4/resolve/main/README.md)
- Serving trap relevant to any per-module calibration: "vLLM fuses in_proj_qkv+z and in_proj_b+a into single NVFP4 GEMMs with one global scale each, while llm-compressor calibrates them per module (up to 2.8× apart). Serving an unharmonized checkpoint silently mis-scales the GDN decay/write gates." — [minima-ai README](https://huggingface.co/minima-ai/mnma_qwen3.8_27b_nvfp4/resolve/main/README.md)

### Inferences
- Consensus per-tensor ranking for Qwen3.5/3.8-27B *weight-only* quantization at ~4 bpw, from most to least bits-per-parameter worth spending: (a) in_proj_a / in_proj_b / conv1d / A_log / dt_bias — keep unquantized (≈24 M params for a/b, negligible); (b) attention k_proj and v_proj (only 2×16×5.2 M = 168 M params; turboderp gives them 5–6 bits at a 4-bpw budget); (c) lm_head (5–6 bits in EXL3; Q6_K/Q8_0 in GGUF); (d) attention o_proj and GDN out_proj (+0.1–0.2 bit average, promoted in early and late layers); (e) in_proj_z (attn_gate) and ffn_down at or slightly above the mean; (f) in_proj_qkv, ffn_up at the mean; (g) q_proj and ffn_gate below the mean (3.6 and 3.96 in turboderp's 4.0 recipe).
- The Thireus/AtomicChat/Bartowski wikitext-type measurements say layer 0 and layers ~52–63 deserve more bits, while turboderp's self-trace-based optimizer demotes layer 0 to 3.6 bpw and promotes 57–61; this is consistent with the exllamav3 doc's claim that the U-shape flattens on self-sampled chat/reasoning data. For a KL objective on agentic traces, the turboderp allocation is the closer analogue; the user should expect layer-0 bits to be less valuable than wikitext-based heuristics suggest, and the last ~7 layers plus layer 63 to remain valuable.
- The Minima result does not contradict the weight-only consensus: it shows the GDN *gates* tolerate 4-bit W4A4 and that error does not compound through the recurrence, so there is no reason to inflate GDN bits on "compounding" grounds; but at a fixed byte budget the degrade-one data still ranks out_proj above ffn tensors per parameter, so a modest promotion (as all three Qwen3.8 recipes do: +0.17 bit EXL3, q5_k/q6_0 in GGUF) remains the empirically-supported choice.
- Qwen's own FP8 ignore list is the authoritative statement of what Qwen considers unsafe at 8-bit block quantization: the small GDN parameters (a/b/ba, conv1d, A_log, dt_bias), norms, embeddings and lm_head. Everything they exclude is tiny except embed/lm_head.

### Gaps
- No published per-tensor KL sweep exists for Qwen3-Next-80B or Qwen3.8-Flash-Next (Bartowski explicitly skipped Flash-Next because of its n-gram PLE table); the Qwen3-Next Unsloth model card contains no sensitivity notes.
- Unsloth's actual per-tensor type map for UD-Q4_K_XL of Qwen3.8-27B could not be read (GGUF header range requests returned 403 in this session); only turboderp's description of UD-Q3_K_XL (IQ3_S/IQ4_XS/Q5_K mix, Q3_K embeddings) is available.
- The Thireus data is a single qtype row (iq1_kt), measured at ctx 512 on a web/wiki-style corpus; sensitivities at 3–5 bits and on long agentic traces may rank differently (exllamav3 doc warns exactly this).
- Nemotron-H-specific per-tensor numbers (e.g. out_proj vs in_proj in Mamba-2 blocks) were only available as snippets; the full NVIDIA AutoQuantize profile table was not retrievable.

## Key Question 3 — Embedding table and lm_head: precision choices, KL cost, asymmetry, 248K-vocabulary head

### Takeaway
Every source that measured it finds a strong asymmetry: the lm_head is one of the most KL-sensitive tensors per parameter (Thireus: degrading it alone to ~1.6 bit costs 0.087 KLD, 5.5× the embedding's 0.016; NVIDIA: "lm_head accounting for nearly half the NVFP4 sensitivity"), whereas the token embedding "is a lookup whose error stays inside one token, so it can be cut hard" (AtomicChat) and "gets a large majority of its performance back from q4_k" (Bartowski). Practitioner choices for Qwen3.8-27B at ~4–4.5 bpw: head 5–6 bits (EXL3 H5/H6, Q6_K, Q8_0), embedding 4–6 bits (iq4_xs, Q3_K, q6_0) or unquantized when VRAM-only accounting is used (turboderp).

### Cited Findings
- exllamav3 defaults: `--head_bits` "Must be an integer from 1 to 8, default is 6"; `--embed_bits` "Must be an integer from 1 to 8, or 16 (the default) to store the table unquantized. Each embedding is split into 256-wide groups that are rotated and trellis-coded without calibration"; "later layers are calibrated against the quantized table"; MTP default 4 bits — [convert.md](https://github.com/turboderp-org/exllamav3/blob/master/doc/convert.md)
- turboderp's published Qwen3.8-27B heads: SC branches use H3 for 1.4–2.2 bpw, H4 for 3.0, H5 for 4.0, H6 for 5.0–6.0; plain branches use H6 at 4.0 bpw; embeddings are stored unquantized (2.4 GiB) in all branches "not counted since they're not loaded into VRAM during inference" — [turboderp README](https://huggingface.co/turboderp/Qwen3.8-27B-exl3/resolve/main/README.md), [discussion #2](https://huggingface.co/turboderp/Qwen3.8-27B-exl3/discussions/2)
- Size arithmetic for the 248,320×5,120 head/embedding (my computation from the recipe JSON): lm_head at 5 bits = 0.741 GiB, at 6 bits = 0.889 GiB, at 4 bits = 0.593 GiB; embedding bf16 = 2.368 GiB. Moving the head from 6 to 5 bits saves 0.148 GiB = 0.047 bpw averaged over the 26.87 B text-model parameters — [SC_4.00bpw_H5 / 4.00bpw quantization_config.json](https://huggingface.co/turboderp/Qwen3.8-27B-exl3/resolve/SC_4.00bpw_H5/quantization_config.json)
- Thireus degrade-one KLD (Qwen3.8-27B, iq1_kt, rest at baseline): output.weight 0.08705 vs token_embd.weight 0.01577 (ratio 5.5×); Qwen3.5-27B: 0.08392 vs 0.01021 (8.2×). The next-worst single tensor is an ffn_up at 0.00894 — [Thireus CSV](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Thireus/GGUF-Tool-Suite/main/models/Qwen3.8-27B/kld_results.csv)
- Bartowski (Qwen3.5-0.8B/4B, degrade-one to q2_k): "token_embd dominates the sensitivity scale ... costing about 8x the worst single weight tensor at 0.8B and 16x at 4B. That said, it gets a large majority of its performance back from q4_k, so on smaller bitrate models it's not necessarily going to be always at q8_0, but it's always going to receive a healthy bump. The old q8_0 embedding versions are gone, replaced with a suggestion to just grab the next highest size"; "The embedding and output tensors are sized by th[e table's share of the file]" (the embedding rule "needed to look at the table's share of the file"). Note: Bartowski's small-model sweep measured token_embd, not output, as dominant (in 0.8B/4B models the embedding is a much larger share of the model) — [Bartowski blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/bartowski/per-tensor-layout-maps-for-gguf-quantization), [bartowski Qwen3.8-27B README](https://huggingface.co/bartowski/Qwen3.8-27B-GGUF/resolve/main/README.md)
- AtomicChat (Qwen3.8-27B): "The token embedding table and the output head weigh the same, 4.7% each, and behave nothing alike. The head decides the next word directly and has to stay precise. The embedding table is a lookup whose error stays inside one token, so it can be cut hard. Paying for the head out of the embedding table is a net gain at every size we tested." Their ablation row "16 lifted, plus a richer output head" went 0.00821→0.00800 for +0.4 GB; final recipes use `output=q6_k`, `token_embd=iq4_xs` — [AtomicChat README](https://huggingface.co/AtomicChat/Qwen3.8-27B-GGUF/resolve/main/README.md)
- ubergarm Qwen3.8-27B IQ4_KS: `token_embd=q6_0`, `output=q8_0` — [ubergarm README](https://huggingface.co/ubergarm/Qwen3.8-27B-GGUF/resolve/main/README.md); ik_llama.cpp "Quant Cookers" guide (dense Qwen3-14B example): token_embd and output at iq6_k — [ik_llama.cpp discussion #434](https://github.com/ikawrakow/ik_llama.cpp/discussions/434)
- Unsloth UD-Q3_K_XL for Qwen3.8-27B quantizes token embeddings to Q3_K (3.44 bpw, ~0.5 GiB) — [turboderp discussion #2](https://huggingface.co/turboderp/Qwen3.8-27B-exl3/discussions/2); AtomicChat's MiniCPM-V-4.7 card reports a Q4_K-with-q8_0-embed/output variant at KLD 0.01972 vs plain Q4_K_M 0.04455 (search snippet) — [AtomicChat MiniCPM-V card (snippet)](https://huggingface.co/AtomicChat/MiniCPM-V-4.7-35B-A3B-GGUF)
- NVIDIA Nemotron (hybrid Mamba-MoE): "lm_head accounting for nearly half the NVFP4 sensitivity" — [Nemotron docs (snippet)](https://docs.nvidia.com/nemotron/latest/nemotron/super35/quantization.html); Qwen3.8-27B-FP8 keeps both `lm_head` and `model.embed_tokens` in BF16 — [config.json](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B-FP8/raw/main/config.json); Minima NVFP4 keeps embeddings and lm_head BF16 — [minima README](https://huggingface.co/minima-ai/mnma_qwen3.8_27b_nvfp4/resolve/main/README.md)
- Practical gotcha: with vLLM's compressed-tensors loader "if the BF16 lm_head isn't in the ignore list ... every draft is rejected — 0 % acceptance" for MTP speculative decoding — [huginnfork FP8 card (snippet)](https://huggingface.co/huginnfork/Qwen3.8-27B-FP8)
- llama.cpp exposes `--output-tensor-type` and `--token-embedding-type`; default `quantize_output_tensor` logic treats `output.weight` and `token_embd.weight` specially (lines 106–125, 896) — [llama-quant.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/master/src/llama-quant.cpp)

### Inferences
- For a 248K-vocab head the per-parameter sensitivity ordering (Thireus: head 0.068 vs embed 0.012 excess KLD per GParam) means that at equal byte cost, 1 extra bit on the head is worth ~5–8× more KL reduction than 1 extra bit on the embedding. A defensible split at a whole-model 4.0–4.5 bpw budget is head 6 bits (Q6_K/EXL3 H6) and embedding 4–5 bits (iq4_xs / EXL3 embed_bits 4–5); dropping the head to 5 bits (turboderp's own choice at 4.0 bpw) is the first thing to trade if the budget is tight, since its per-bit KL cost at K≥5 is small while 0.148 GiB is recovered.
- Whole-text-model bpw arithmetic for the user (my computation; 24.327 B layers + 1.271 B head + 1.271 B embed): EXL3 layers at 4.00 + H5 + bf16 embed = 4.62 bpw; with embed at 4 bits → 4.05; at 6 bits → 4.15; at 8 bits → 4.24. With H6 instead: embed 4 → 4.10, embed 6 → 4.19, embed 8 → 4.29. So "4.0–4.5 bpw including embed and head" is reachable only if the embedding is quantized (≤8 bits) — a bf16 embedding alone pushes the average to 4.6+.
- Because exllamav3 quantizes the embedding without calibration but calibrates later layers against the quantized table, the KL cost of a 4–6-bit embedding is partly absorbed downstream; this matches AtomicChat's and Bartowski's empirical "cut the embedding hard" results.

### Gaps
- No source reports a controlled KL curve for the *same* 27B model with head at 8/6/5/4 bits and everything else fixed; the closest are AtomicChat's single ablation row (+0.4 GB richer head → −2.6 % KLD) and turboderp's H4/H5/H6 choices (no per-choice KL published).
- No source reports KL for 8- vs 6- vs 4-bit *embeddings* on Qwen3.8-27B specifically; the embedding findings are from Qwen3.5-0.8B/4B (Bartowski) and qualitative statements (AtomicChat).
- exllamav3 "Options for token-embedding quantization" (v1.5.4) exist but no published EXL3 quant of Qwen3.8-27B with a quantized embedding and KL numbers was found.

## Key Question 4 — Effective-bitrate accounting across formats

### Takeaway
At "nominal 4 bits" the real storage cost ranges from 4.004–4.02 bpw (EXL3: trellis + two fp16 Hadamard sign/scale vectors per tensor) through 4.125–4.16 (int4 g128 fp16 scale ± 4-bit zero), 4.25 (IQ4_XS, MXFP4, ik IQ4_KS), 4.5 (Q4_K, Q4_0, IQ4_NL, NVFP4) to ~4.85–5.0 for a llama.cpp "Q4_K_M" *mix*; file-level bpw numbers published by quantizers (e.g. Bartowski Q4_K_M = 5.02 bpw, ubergarm IQ4_KS mix = 4.731) include embed/head/MTP and the fp32 norms, so compare at the file level only after deciding what is counted.

### Cited Findings
- EXL3 stored-tensor layout (primary; from turboderp's recipe JSON): each quantized Linear stores `trellis` (int16-packed, e.g. shape [384,320,80] = 19,660,800 bytes for out_proj 6144×5120 at 5 bits), `suh` (fp16, length = in_features), `svh` (fp16, length = out_features) and `mul1` (one int32). Effective bpw = nominal + (in+out)×16/(in×out): 4.004 for 5120×17408 MLP tensors, 4.005–4.006 for 5120×10240/6144 GDN tensors, 4.019 for k/v_proj 5120×1024; the layers-only mean of the plain 4.00bpw branch is 4.0045 — [4.00bpw quantization_config.json](https://huggingface.co/turboderp/Qwen3.8-27B-exl3/resolve/4.00bpw/quantization_config.json)
- turboderp: "'3.00 bpw' in EXL3 terms is literal and means the model layers are quantized to almost exactly 3 bits per weight (with the occasional tiny norm layer in FP16 etc.)"; a third-party benchmark table lists EXL3 "4.00 bpw" with layer BPW 4.006 and "3.00 bpw" at 3.006 — [discussion #2](https://huggingface.co/turboderp/Qwen3.8-27B-exl3/discussions/2), [AtomicChat EXL3 vs GGUF (snippet)](https://atomic.chat/blog/guides/exl3-vs-gguf)
- Whole-file EXL3 accounting (Qwen3.8-27B, 3.00 bpw SC): "text model layers + output head: ~9.4 GiB; embeddings: ~2.4 GiB; mtp: ~0.1 GiB; vision: ~0.9 GiB; total: ~12.8 GiB = ~13.8 GB" — [discussion #2](https://huggingface.co/turboderp/Qwen3.8-27B-exl3/discussions/2); plain 4.00bpw branch safetensors total 16.86 GB incl. bf16 embed, bf16 vision and MTP; SC_4.00bpw_H5 stored-tensor total 14.49 GiB (text + bf16 embed, excl. vision) — [HF tree API / recipe JSON](https://huggingface.co/api/models/turboderp/Qwen3.8-27B-exl3/tree/4.00bpw)
- GGUF type bitrates: turboderp lists IQ3_S 3.44, Q3_K 3.44, IQ4_XS 4.25, Q5_K 5.5 bpw — [discussion #2](https://huggingface.co/turboderp/Qwen3.8-27B-exl3/discussions/2); Thireus' built-in table: f32 32, q8_0 8.5, iq4_nl 4.5, iq3_xxs 3.0625, iq2_xs 2.3125, iq1_s 1.5625 — [quant_assign.md](https://github.com/Thireus/GGUF-Tool-Suite/blob/main/docs/quant_assign.md); ubergarm's file-level figures: BF16 16.002 BPW, Q8_0 8.502 BPW, IQ4_KS mix 4.731 BPW (15.749 GiB) — [ubergarm README](https://huggingface.co/ubergarm/Qwen3.8-27B-GGUF/resolve/main/README.md); Bartowski's file-level bpw for Qwen3.8-27B: Q4_K_L 5.42, Q4_K_M 5.02, IQ4_XS 4.46 — [bartowski README](https://huggingface.co/bartowski/Qwen3.8-27B-GGUF/resolve/main/README.md)
- Why a "Q4_K_M" is ~5 bpw: llama.cpp promotes attn_v and ffn_down to Q6_K in `use_more_bits` layers, output to Q6_K, and K-quant superblocks of 256 carry 6-bit sub-scales/mins (Q4_K = 4.5 bpw) — [llama-quant.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/master/src/llama-quant.cpp); Bartowski: "If a file that starts with Q3_K_ is mostly non-Q3_K tensor types and sits above 5 bits per weight, something has gone wrong" and the new tiers fix "bpw collapse" — [Bartowski blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/bartowski/per-tensor-layout-maps-for-gguf-quantization)
- Block-floating formats: NVFP4 = 16 × 4-bit E2M1 + one E4M3 scale per block → "72 / 16 = 4.50" bits/element (plus a negligible per-tensor fp32 scale); MXFP4 = 32-element blocks with an E8M0 scale → 4.25 bpw — [NVFP4 explainer (snippet)](https://www.zeroentropy.dev/concepts/nvfp4/), [NVIDIA TE NVFP4 docs (snippet)](https://docs.nvidia.com/deeplearning/transformer-engine/user-guide/features/low_precision_training/nvfp4/nvfp4.html)
- Minima's all-NVFP4 Qwen3.8-27B is 17.53 GiB in VRAM with BF16 embed/lm_head; BF16 is 50.13 GiB — [minima README](https://huggingface.co/minima-ai/mnma_qwen3.8_27b_nvfp4/resolve/main/README.md)
- The GGUF tiled layout of GDN projections matters for loaders: "IQ-quantized Gated DeltaNet projections are loaded as F16 (+7.3 GiB on a 27B IQ3_S)" in one third-party engine because "the reordering splits 256-element IQ blocks"; ssm_out alone is 1.32 B parameters — [xinfer issue #510 (snippet)](https://github.com/guoqingbao/xinfer/issues/510)

### Inferences
- Fair comparison table at nominal 4 bits for a 5120×17408 tensor (arithmetic from the cited block definitions): EXL3 4.0 → 4.004 bpw; int4 g128 + fp16 scale → 4.125; int4 g128 + fp16 scale + 4-bit zero (GPTQ/AWQ style) → 4.156; IQ4_XS / MXFP4 / ik IQ4_KS → 4.25; Q4_K / Q4_0 / IQ4_NL / NVFP4 → 4.5. Thus "EXL3 4.0" has ~11 % fewer bytes than Q4_K and ~6 % fewer than IQ4_XS for the same nominal width; turboderp's KL-vs-VRAM charts compare on bytes, which is why Unsloth UD-Q3_K_XL (~3.9 bpw avg) lands near EXL3 3.0 on his plot.
- For the user's objective (fixed byte budget over the whole text model), the only honest denominator is total bytes ÷ 26.87 B parameters including embed + head + fp32/bf16 norms (norms are ~0.5 M params per layer, negligible), and the only honest numerator is the file minus vision tower and minus MTP (or with MTP declared).

### Gaps

## Key Question 5 — Do errors in recurrent/linear-attention layers compound over long sequences more than attention-layer errors (and so deserve more bits)?

### Takeaway
The only direct test on Qwen3.8-27B (Minima, Sep 2026) says no: with all GDN projections at NVFP4 W4A4, injected noise "holds at a flat plateau over 32K tokens", a state impulse is forgotten "within hundreds of steps" because each delta-rule write overwrites the state along the current key direction, and the perplexity gap *shrinks* from +0.73 at 4K to +0.15 at 32K. Compounding is real for quantizing the *recurrent state itself* (DAMP, STEPQuant, Nemotron 3 Super's SSM-cache note), which is a KV-cache-analogue decision, not a weight-bit decision; and generic long-context studies on softmax-attention models show weight-quantization KL growing with context length, so nothing supports giving GDN *weights* extra bits on compounding grounds.

### Cited Findings
- Minima (Qwen3.8-27B, authors): "(iii) the delta-rule recurrence holds injected noise at a flat plateau over 32K tokens and forgets a state impulse within hundreds of steps, because each write overwrites the state along the current key direction; (iv) the per-token quantization cost washes out with context instead of compounding"; "its 32K perplexity gap shrinks with position"; PPL gap +0.73 @4K → +0.15 @32K; RULER NIAH 100 % at 32K/64K — [HF paper page 2609.04098](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.04098), [minima README](https://huggingface.co/minima-ai/mnma_qwen3.8_27b_nvfp4/resolve/main/README.md)
- The same paper names the prior it refutes: early community quants "left the GDN block in 8- or 16-bit precision -- especially its decay and write-strength gates -- on the intuition that errors in a recurrence accumulate over long contexts" — [HF paper page](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.04098)
- Recurrent-*state* quantization (different object: the fixed-size state, not weights): DAMP (arXiv 2608.27513, Aug 2026) "uniform quantization provides a poor accuracy–storage trade-off: INT8 and FP8 already degrade accuracy on complex reasoning tasks, while INT4 and NVFP4 reduce it to near zero", with risk channels identified by "quantization-error energy and decay-based persistence" — [HF paper page](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.27513); STEPQuant (arXiv 2609.38169, tested on Qwen3.8-27B): "quantization errors propagate through successive state updates ... temporally, errors in long-lived memory can persist across many decoding steps"; 6-bit states "closely matches FP32-state accuracy" — [HF paper page](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.38169)
- Nemotron 3 Super report: "directly casting the SSM cache to FP16 can result in up to 40% increase in verbosity when combined with W8A8 quantization", attributed to error accumulating over recurrent decoding steps — [arXiv 2604.12374 (snippet)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.12374)
- Generic (softmax-attention) long-context evidence: "Evaluating Zero-Shot Long-Context LLM Compression" (arXiv 2406.06773, LLaMA-2-7B-32K) models compression as hidden-state noise whose error "grows with context length", finds "quantization degraded more with length than pruning", and that keeping ~2 % of high-magnitude weights at 8-bit reduces the KL growth — [arXiv 2406.06773 (snippet)](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2406.06773); a 100K-token tool-calling benchmark write-up reports KL "clusters at specific later prompt positions" rather than rising smoothly (blog, unverified) — [mer.vin (snippet)](https://mer.vin/news/local-llm-quantization-benchmarks-reveal-silent-tool-call-failures/)
- exllamav3's own measurement protocol for its KL objective is over "the sampled response positions only" of a self-generated trace, with per-token KLD vectors retained — [optimize.md](https://github.com/turboderp-org/exllamav3/blob/master/doc/optimize.md); AtomicChat measured at 4096-token context on an in-domain set that is 24.7 % agentic/tool-use, 17.8 % code, 11.9 % long context — [AtomicChat README](https://huggingface.co/AtomicChat/Qwen3.8-27B-GGUF/resolve/main/README.md)
- Nemotron 3 Nano's sensitivity study found the Mamba layers that "feed into the self-attention layers" sensitive (kept BF16) — i.e. the interaction point, not the recurrence per se — [arXiv 2512.20848 (snippet)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.20848)

### Inferences
- For the user's 256K-token agentic-trace KL metric, the weight-side evidence points to spending bits where per-token sensitivity is highest (k/v, head, o_proj/out_proj, late layers) rather than inflating GDN projections for compounding reasons; the Minima result (error forgotten within hundreds of steps) implies GDN weight error behaves like a per-token perturbation, and the Qwen3.8 FP8 config shows Qwen itself quantizes in_proj_qkv/in_proj_z/out_proj.
- Where compounding genuinely bites is the recurrent state precision at inference (keep fp32/bf16 state; do not cast the SSM cache to low precision) and the KV cache of the 16 attention layers; these are inference-engine settings that should be held fixed when measuring KL of weight recipes.
- The one place a conservative allocation still makes sense is the GDN layer immediately preceding each attention layer (Nemotron 3 Nano's finding) — in Qwen3.8-27B these are layers 2,6,10,...,62; no Qwen-specific test of this hypothesis was found.

### Gaps
- No study measures per-position KL as a function of context length on Qwen3.8-27B for *weight-only* 4-bit quants (EXL3/GGUF) with GDN vs attention tensors varied independently; Minima's evidence is for NVFP4 W4A4 and perplexity, not top-512-coarsened KL on agentic traces.
- The 256K regime is unmeasured anywhere: Minima goes to 64K (RULER) and 32K (PPL); AtomicChat 4K; GGUF KLD benchmarks 512.
- Whether exllamav3's `sc_measure` sensitivities change materially if the trace rows are 32K+ tokens instead of 2048 (default `-cc 2048`) is not documented.
