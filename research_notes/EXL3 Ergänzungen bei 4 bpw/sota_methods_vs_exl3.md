# State of the art in weight-only PTQ at 3.5–5 bpw versus EXL3 (QTIP-derived trellis quantisation), as of 2026-10-28

Scope note on sources: arxiv.org, unsloth.ai, atomic.chat, alphaxiv, openreview and most blogs were not reachable from this session (network allow-list), so paper tables were obtained through search-engine excerpts, GitHub READMEs/raw files (reachable), Hugging Face model cards and the HF discussions API (reachable), and a local clone of `turboderp-org/exllamav3` (charts in `doc/` read as images). Where a number was read off a chart rather than a table, this is stated. "Author-reported" vs "third-party" is marked on every number.

Reminder of the user's competition baselines (score = mean KL of next-token distribution vs bf16 on a mixed agentic/code/chat/reasoning corpus, size = bytes of the text model incl. embeddings, 64 layers and lm_head, on Qwen3.8-27B): GPTQ+Hadamard-128 mixed 5/4-bit at 4.457 bpw -> KL 0.00904; Unsloth UD-Q4_K_S 4.461 bpw -> 0.01057; 5/4/3-bit VQ mix 4.137 bpw -> 0.01327; Unsloth UD-IQ4_XS 4.132 bpw -> 0.01499.

## Key question 1: How do QTIP/EXL3, QuIP#, AQLM, PV-tuning, GPTVQ, VPTQ, HIGGS, GPTQ+rotation, AWQ and llama.cpp K-/I-quants compare at ~4 bpw (actual numbers, model named)?

### Takeaway
On every like-for-like measurement found, QTIP/EXL3 at 4.0 bpw has roughly half the KL of the best llama.cpp 4-bit quants that are 0.3–0.8 bpw *larger* (e.g. Llama 3.1 8B Instruct: EXL3 4.0 bpw KL 0.013 vs GGUF Q4_K_M-imatrix 0.018 at ~4.8 bpw and IQ4_XS-imatrix 0.023 at ~4.3 bpw; Qwen3.8-Flash-Next: EXL3 4.05 bpw 0.0067 vs UD-IQ4_XS 0.0165 at the same bpw). VPTQ at 4.5 bpw lands near Q4_K_M (0.024), AQLM/PV-tuning only publish ≤3-bit points, and AWQ 4-bit sits well behind on the 70B perplexity curve. None of the classical VQ/lattice methods (QuIP#, AQLM, GPTVQ, VPTQ, HIGGS) report numbers that beat QTIP at 4 bits.

### Inferences
- The EXL3-vs-GGUF gap at ~4 bpw is consistently ~2x in KL on dense models (Llama 8B: 0.013 vs 0.018 at 0.8 bpw *less*; Gemma 4: 0.0248 vs 0.0398) and ~2.5x on the Qwen 3.8 hybrid MoE (0.0067 vs 0.0165 at equal bpw). The user's UD-IQ4_XS baseline scores 0.01499 on the competition metric; a plain EXL3 4.0-bpw-class quant should therefore plausibly land in the ~0.006–0.009 range *on that metric* if the ratio transfers, i.e. at or just below the current leader (GPTQ+Hadamard-128 mixed 5/4-bit, 0.00904 at 4.457 bpw) at ~0.3 bpw less. This is an extrapolation; nobody has published EXL3 KL on Qwen3.8-27B on a comparable corpus.
- The fact that the leader is a *GPTQ-class scalar* quant with Hadamard-128 rotation at 0.009 and the 5/4/3-bit VQ mix is only 0.0133 suggests the competition's winners so far are mostly winning on allocation (mixed 5/4-bit), not on codebook quality. A trellis quantiser at 4.0–4.5 bpw has ~0.5–1.0 bpw of "free" headroom relative to those entries.
- The 248k vocabulary matters for size accounting: embed + head = 2 x 248320 x 5120 ≈ 2.54 B params ≈ 9.5% of the 26.9 B total. Every bit on those two tensors costs ~0.095 bpw of the challenge's size axis; EXL3's default head at 6 bits and bf16 embeddings (16 bits) would alone cost ~1.0 bpw, so an EXL3-style entry must quantise embeddings (`--embed_bits`, supported since the current convert.py) and the head to ~4 bits to compete.

### Gaps
- No published EXL3 (or QTIP) KL number on Qwen3.8-27B dense from turboderp; the model card only has charts hosted on `cdn-uploads.huggingface.co`, which was unreachable. The card lists self-calibrated ("SC") variants at 1.40–6.00 bpw with head bits H3–H6 and plain variants 2.00–6.00 bpw. — [turboderp/Qwen3.8-27B-exl3](https://huggingface.co/turboderp/Qwen3.8-27B-exl3)
- QTIP paper's exact 4-bit perplexity table cells (Llama 2/3) could not be retrieved (arXiv blocked; excerpts only gave the 2-bit distortion table).
- No 4-bit KL numbers exist for AQLM, PV-tuning, GPTVQ, VPTQ, HIGGS from their authors; their papers target ≤3 bits.

## Key question 2: Are there newer (2025–2026) methods that beat QTIP/EXL3 at 4 bpw (YAQA, ParoQuant, M2XFP, NVFP4/MXFP4 with learned metadata, learned-codebook trellis/lattice, 2026 preprints)? What do they claim?

### Takeaway
The only method with an author-reported, like-for-like KL improvement *over QTIP at 4 bits* is YAQA (Tseng, Sun, De Sa, ICML 2026): a Kronecker-factored end-to-end-KL Hessian for the rounding step, ~30% lower KL than LDLQ/GPTQ rounding on Llama 3.1 70B Instruct at 4 bits (chart: ≈0.045 -> ≈0.030 on wikitext2/8K) and below Google's Gemma 3 12B QAT. Crucially, exllamav3 (v1.6.x) already ships a YAQA-sketch-A implementation (`util/yaqa_hessians.py`, `--hessians`, two-sided LDLQ `ldlq_2hess`, `--out_scales yaqa`), plus a per-tensor sensitivity/recipe optimiser (`sc_measure.py`/`sc_optimize.py`) and in-domain self-generated calibration. Everything else found (ParoQuant, GSQ, SLQ, CurveTQ, HyperQuant, GLVQ, M2XFP, NVFP4 scale-learning) either targets scalar/INT4 formats, ≤3 bits, or hardware co-design, and none claims to beat QTIP at 4 bits on KL.

### Cited Findings

**YAQA (arXiv 2505.22988, "Model-Preserving Adaptive Rounding", ICML 2026)**
- Method: adaptive rounding using Kronecker-factored approximations of each layer's Hessian w.r.t. the *full-model* KL (Fisher information), quantiser-agnostic (works with QTIP or INT4); two sketches, A (tokens independent, cheaper) and B (one power-iteration step, better); claims "reduces the KL divergence to the original model by a factor of 1/3 over LDLQ/GPTQ across a wide range of models and quantizers" and "achieves a lower KL than QAT". — [Cornell-RelaxML/yaqa README](https://github.com/Cornell-RelaxML/yaqa); [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22988v1); [ICML 2026 slides](https://icml.cc/media/icml-2026/Slides/64269.pdf) (search excerpt)
- Numbers read off the official comparison chart (`assets/comp.png`, "Calculated on Wikitext2, 8K context length"): Llama 3.1 70B Instruct, QTIP (LDLQ rounding) vs YAQA rounding: 2-bit ≈0.50 vs ≈0.34; 3-bit ≈0.14 vs ≈0.094; **4-bit ≈0.045 vs ≈0.030**. Gemma 3 12B Instruct 4-bit: Google QAT ≈0.089 vs YAQA ≈0.057. — [yaqa/assets/comp.png](https://github.com/Cornell-RelaxML/yaqa/blob/main/assets/comp.png) (chart; values approximate)
- Pre-quantised YAQA+QTIP Llama 3.1 70B Instruct at 2/3/4 bit are on HF (relaxml/Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct-{2,3,4}Bit-YAQA-QTIP). — [HF relaxml](https://huggingface.co/relaxml)
- Third-party review: on Gemma 3 12B "the QAT model may score slightly better on downstream metrics, but YAQA's quantized models sit closer to the original model in KL". — [themoonlight review](https://www.themoonlight.io/review/model-preserving-adaptive-rounding)

**YAQA inside exllamav3 (author = turboderp; code read from the cloned repo, v1.6.5, 2026-10-25)**
- `util/yaqa_hessians.py`: "Kronecker-factored Hessians for two-sided LDLQ, after YAQA (Tseng, Sun, De Sa 2025, arXiv 2505.22988), sketch A." Default collects only the output-side factor: "On Qwen3.5-2B the whole benefit came from the output side: the online (quantized-stream) Hin of a regular conversion together with this Hout beat the fully iterated pair." Sampling noise: "Hout ... two seeds differ by ~20% (Frobenius) at 250 rows, ~40% at 64 ... At 250 rows the quantized model doesn't seem to care." Covers q/k/v/o, gate/up/down, GDN `in_proj_qkv|in_proj_z|in_proj_qkvz|out_proj`; "Small projections that EXL3 leaves unquantized (GDN a/b, routers etc.) are not matched"; fused MoE expert tensors not covered. — [exllamav3 util/yaqa_hessians.py](https://github.com/turboderp-org/exllamav3/blob/master/util/yaqa_hessians.py)
- `doc/convert.md`: `-hess/--hessians` ("Files with an output-side factor (hout) switch the rounding of that tensor to two-sided LDLQ"), `-hreg` (default 0.025, "Larger values blend toward one-sided LDLQ"), `-h1` (keep one-sided LDLQ, use hout only for output scales), `--out_scales yaqa` ("scales follow how much the model's output depends on each output channel ... so the quantization error lands in the channels where it costs the least"); `--out_scales auto` drops scales on gate/up of gated MLPs "where calibration statistics show that the channels the MLP's output is most sensitive to are also the ones with the largest weights"; `-eb/--embed_bits` (trellis-coded embedding table, decoded on GPU); codebook `mul1` default (int8 GEMV paths), `mcg`/`3inst` legacy. — [exllamav3 doc/convert.md](https://github.com/turboderp-org/exllamav3/blob/master/doc/convert.md)
- `exl3_lib/quantize.py`: `ldlq_2hess` = "LDLQ with error feedback along both axes, for a Kronecker-factored Hessian H ~ H_out (x) H_in (YAQA)... objective tr(E^T H_in E H_out) ... 16x16 tiles quantized along anti-diagonals". — [exllamav3 quantize.py](https://github.com/turboderp-org/exllamav3/blob/master/exllamav3/modules/quant/exl3_lib/quantize.py)
- `doc/optimize.md` (per-tensor recipe pipeline): "measured on a model's own chat/reasoning output, the familiar U-shape (first/last layers hottest) largely flattens, and small mid-stack attention projections (k/v) become the most valuable bits"; "Sensitivities measured on raw text transfer poorly ... Sensitivities measured on a self-sampled trace generalize much better"; noise model `kld(t,K) = S_t * rfn_t(K)^alpha`, alpha ≈2 ("real-quant transitions measure ~2.0"), `--alpha_low` "Measured ~2.35 for the K2->K1 octave on Qwen3.8-27B"; shaped (LDLQ-distributed) noise injection validates at Spearman 0.84 vs ground-truth swap attribution (0.61 unshaped), "isotropic noise overestimates sensitivity by up to ~6x on tensors with strongly anisotropic input statistics (v_proj tends to be worst)"; rfn anchors "typically a ±10% spread at fixed K"; a full run for a ~27B dense model "takes roughly two hours on a single fast GPU"; ties `k_proj+v_proj, gate_proj+up_proj`. Evaluation: `eval/qbench.py` KLD over sampled response positions vs bf16, held-out self-generated trace with 30% tool-calling rows. — [exllamav3 doc/optimize.md](https://github.com/turboderp-org/exllamav3/blob/master/doc/optimize.md)
- turboderp's Qwen3.8-27B card publishes the calibration trace (217 rows, 103,662 input + 509,290 output tokens, thinking enabled, reasoning_effort medium, vocab 248077) and the held-out eval trace (24 rows, 19,016 + 45,930 tokens). — [turboderp/Qwen3.8-27B-exl3 cal_trace.md](https://huggingface.co/turboderp/Qwen3.8-27B-exl3/blob/main/cal_trace.md)

**ParoQuant (arXiv 2511.10645, ICLR 2026; NVIDIA/MIT/UCSD)**
- Pairwise independent Givens rotations + channel-wise scaling, INT4 weight-only, group size 128; "average 2.4% accuracy improvement over AWQ on reasoning tasks with less than 10% overhead"; "retaining only the top-10% channel pairs suffices to match full rotation". A `z-lab/Qwen3.8-27B-PARO` 4-bit checkpoint exists; the card gives no KL/perplexity. No comparison to QTIP/EXL3 found. — [arXiv 2511.10645](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.10645); [z-lab/paroquant README](https://github.com/z-lab/paroquant); [z-lab/Qwen3.8-27B-PARO](https://huggingface.co/z-lab/Qwen3.8-27B-PARO)

**M2XFP (arXiv 2601.19213, ASPLOS 2026) and NVFP4/MXFP4 scale-learning**
- M2XFP is an algorithm-*hardware* co-design (cs.AR): metadata-augmented microscaling format with online quantisation; "70.63% reduction in accuracy loss compared to MXFP4 and 37.30% relative to NVFP4", 1.91x speedup over SOTA accelerators. Requires custom hardware; not a software weight format comparable to EXL3/GGUF. — [arXiv 2601.19213](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.19213)
- MR-GPTQ (Egiazarian et al., ICLR 2026): rotations help MXFP4 but "hurt NVFP4 accuracy when coupled with RTN"; MR-GPTQ brings MXFP4 within ~1–2% of NVFP4; "FP4 is not an automatic upgrade over INT4". — [arXiv 2509.23202](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23202)

None reports KL against QTIP. — [FOCUS](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.01847), [SOAR](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.12245), [H-Scale](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.28113.md)

- Q-Palette (NeurIPS 2025, SNU): fractional-bit TCQ/VQ/NUQ quantisers with fused kernels; TCQ "near-optimal for Gaussian sources"; mixed-scheme allocation; claims superior wikitext2 ppl vs HQQ/NormalFloat/*data-free* QTIP; 28–36% speedups. - CurveTQ (arXiv 2610.09212, Oct 2026, Black Sesame): rotation-free trellis quantisation with per-coordinate curvature weights from the Hessian LDL factorisation; costs 14 bits per 64-column block (~0.22 bpw); at 2 bits "+1–3 points mean downstream accuracy vs QTIP and Proteus on three 4–8B Instruct models"; Qwen3-4B-Instruct 51.8% at 2.29 bpw vs QTIP 49.8% at 2.19 bpw (not equal-rate). No 3/4-bit or KL numbers found. — [alphaxiv 2610.09212](https://alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.09212v1) (search excerpt)
- "XOR-Trellis" (arXiv 2610.00432, Oct 2026): reported Llama-3.1-8B wikitext2 ppl 8.00 vs 8.48 for RHT-enabled QTIP (bit-width not captured). A second search could not find a paper of that title; treat as unverified. — [arXiv 2610.00432](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.00432) (search excerpt only)
- BCJR-QAT (arXiv 2605.10655, May 2026): differentiable (BCJR) relaxation of the Viterbi argmax so QAT can be done on a trellis quantiser — QAT, not PTQ. — [arXiv 2605.10655](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10655)
- GLVQ (arXiv 2510.20984): learned grouped lattice VQ; 2-bit Llama-2-70B 3.36 vs QTIP 3.78. No 4-bit. — [arXiv 2510.20984](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.20984)
- "Initialisation Determines the Basin" (arXiv 2604.08118): learned codebooks for extreme quantisation; authors state it "does not directly transfer to lattice-based (QuIP#, GLVQ) or trellis-based (QTIP) methods". — [arXiv 2604.08118](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.08118v1)
- HyperQuant (arXiv 2606.23406, June 2026): per-tile RHT + optimal lattices (E8 > D4 > A2 > Z) + entropy coding + bias correction; "at 4 bps on an H100 ... near-lossless"; beats HIGGS at every point 3–5 bps; no QTIP comparison. — [HF paper page](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.23406)

### Inferences
- For the user's goal (beat 0.00904 at ≤4.5 bpw on Qwen3.8-27B), the strongest *published* stack is exactly what exllamav3 already implements: QTIP-style trellis (mul1 codebook) + YAQA output-side Hessians (two-sided LDLQ, `--out_scales yaqa`) + in-domain self-sampled calibration (`sc_trace.py`) + per-tensor recipe (`sc_measure.py`/`sc_optimize.py`) + quantised embeddings/head. ## Key question 4: Results on Gated DeltaNet / Mamba-style hybrids (Qwen3-Next, Qwen3.5/3.6/3.8, Nemotron-H)? Known sensitivities (linear-attention output projection, state-space gates)?

### Takeaway
No academic quantisation paper evaluates on Qwen3-Next/3.5/3.8 or Nemotron-H with per-layer-type sensitivities; the closest is a 2026 KL-sensitivity study on the Hymba SSM-attention hybrid (Mamba `x_proj` > 10x more sensitive than any other projection; `dt_proj` nearly insensitive; one late block dominates). Practitioner evidence on Qwen3.5/3.8 agrees that the GDN small gate projections (`ssm_alpha`/`ssm_beta`, i.e. `in_proj_a/b`) must stay at ≥8 bit (llama.cpp and EXL3 leave them unquantised), that `ssm_out` should not be pushed below ~4–6 bit, and that MXFP4 was markedly worse than Q4_K on `attn_gate/attn_q/ssm_alpha/ssm_beta`. Crucially, a size-matched experiment on Qwen3.8-27B found that funding extra protection of the GDN path *out of the FFN* loses (FFN is ~64% of weights), and turboderp's own optimiser finds mid-stack k/v the most valuable bits on in-domain data.

## Key question 5: Theoretical headroom — Gaussian rate-distortion at 4 bits, how close trellis/VQ methods get, and per-layer reconstruction vs end-to-end error

### Takeaway
For an i.i.d. Gaussian source the Shannon bound is D(R)=2^{-2R}, i.e. MSE 2^{-8}=0.0039 (−24.1 dB) at 4 bits; TCQ with QTIP's trellis sits within ~0.1 dB (≈0.08 dB at 3 bits per a third-party summary; 0.071 vs 0.063 = +0.5 dB at 2 bits in QTIP's own table) while optimal 2D VQ is ~0.5 dB and scalar/K-quant style quantisers ~1.2–1.5 dB (≈0.25 bit) above it. So at 4 bpw the *codebook* headroom over EXL3 is ≲0.1 dB (a few percent MSE); the remaining error is dominated by (a) how MSE in the rotated domain maps to output KL (rounding objective: LDLQ vs YAQA's end-to-end Fisher, worth ~30% KL) and (b) bit allocation across tensors, which multiple 2026 works show is spread across roughly half the network rather than a few layers.

### Cited Findings
- QTIP's own Gaussian distortion comparison at 2 bits: scalar 0.118, QuIP# E8P 0.089, QTIP HYB 0.071, optimal 0.063 (=2^{-4}); TCQ (Marcellin & Fischer 1989) approaches the rate-distortion function as trellis size L -> inf. — [QTIP paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.11235) (search excerpt)
- Q-Palette: "fine-grained fractional-bit quantizers approaching the Gaussian distortion-rate bound are essential"; decomposes the gap into a distortion gap per bitwidth and a bit-allocation gap; TCQ "near-optimal for Gaussian sources". Third-party summary (unverified): 3-bit gaps to bound: scalar +1.23 dB, 2D-VQ +0.52 dB, TCQ +0.08 dB. — [arXiv 2509.20214](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.20214); [papernotes](https://en.papernotes.org/NeurIPS2025/model_compression/q-palette_fractional-bit_quantizers_toward_optimal_bit_allocation_for_efficient_/)
- WaterSIC (MIT MEng thesis 2026, Polyanskiy group): proves a rate gap ≤ ≈0.255 bits to the information-theoretic bound "uniformly over all input covariance matrices and at all rates" (the standard scalar-uniform vs optimal-VQ gap), and that GPTQ "may have an arbitrarily large rate gap ... depending on the conditioning of the input covariance"; experiments on Llama/Qwen at 1–4 bpw. — [thesis PDF](https://people.lids.mit.edu/yp/homepage/data/theses/2026_MEng_Lifar.pdf) (search excerpt)
- HyperQuant: lattice SNR orders E8 > D4 > A2 > Z "exactly as the textbook normalized second moments"; uses RHT to make weights ≈Gaussian; argues "equal weight SNR implies equal expected PPL hit regardless of error shape" (calibrated SNR-to-bits table instead of per-config ppl). — [HF paper page 2606.23406](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.23406) (search excerpt)
- "The Great Inversion": LLM quantisation benefits from *spreading* energy (Hadamard cuts crest factor 3.7 -> 1.9); non-orthogonal whitening+flattening transforms (WUSH) are strongest. — [arXiv 2608.25188](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.25188) (search excerpt)
- YAQA: the rounding objective matters ~30% in KL at fixed quantiser; LDLQ/GPTQ minimise layer-input-weighted error, YAQA minimises a Kronecker (H_out ⊗ H_in) approximation of the end-to-end KL Hessian; sketches reach much higher cosine similarity to the true Hessian than LDLQ's. — [YAQA README](https://github.com/Cornell-RelaxML/yaqa); [ICML slides excerpt](https://icml.cc/media/icml-2026/Slides/64269.pdf)
- turboderp's empirical law: per-tensor KLD contribution `S_t * rfn_t(K)^alpha` with alpha ≈ 2 (quadratic in relative weight error), steepening to ~2.35 below the 2-bit anchor on Qwen3.8-27B; rfn halves per bit ("amplitude-halving law"); LDLQ "steers error away from directions the data exercises" so isotropic-noise sensitivity overestimates by up to ~6x on anisotropic tensors (v_proj worst). — [exllamav3 doc/optimize.md](https://github.com/turboderp-org/exllamav3/blob/master/doc/optimize.md)
- "The Structure of Quantization Damage in LLMs: Why the Next Bit Should Be Spent Globally" (arXiv 2609.01587): no single layer accounts for more than ~44% of damage in any of 9 models; protecting boundary layers recovers ≤13% of the gap in 6/9 models; "recovering most of the gap thus needs roughly half the network, not a few layers". — [arXiv 2609.01587](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.01587) (search excerpt)
- Quantization Error Propagation (QEP, arXiv 2504.09629): "accumulation of quantization errors across layers significantly degrades performance, particularly in low-bit regimes"; proposes explicit propagation/compensation. GPTAQ's "asymmetric calibration" (match the FP model's layer output rather than the already-quantised stream) is the same idea. — [arXiv 2504.09629](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09629); [GPTAQ](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02692v3)
- SLQ: zero-shot accuracy is flat for KL ≲ 0.01 and drops beyond; "distribution-lossless" (EAR ≥ 0.99) needs 5.0–6.6 bpp with GPTQ-INT scalar quantisation. — [IST-DASLab/SLQ](https://github.com/IST-DASLab/SLQ)
- Measurement floor: turboderp's Qwen3.8-Flash-Next chart shows a "noise floor, mean: 0.00249" (bf16 vs bf16 run-to-run/numerics), and EXL3 6.05 bpw at 0.0031 is essentially at it; bartowski's Q8_0 on Qwen3.8-27B is 0.0009 on wikitext. — [exllamav3 README chart](https://github.com/turboderp-org/exllamav3); [bartowski](https://huggingface.co/bartowski/Qwen3.8-27B-GGUF)

### Inferences
- At 4 bpw the trellis codebook itself is within a few percent of the Gaussian bound; the ~2x KL advantage of EXL3 over K-quants at equal bpw is consistent with K-quants' ~1.2–1.5 dB (≈0.25–0.3 bit) distortion gap plus block-scale overhead, and with alpha ≈ 2 (a 1.3 dB MSE gap -> ~1.35x KL, the rest coming from allocation and calibration). The remaining levers above the codebook, in rough order of expected KL impact on the competition metric, are: (1) in-domain calibration/Hessians (turboderp: wikitext-measured recipes "transfer poorly"); (2) end-to-end rounding objective (YAQA, ~30%); (3) measured per-tensor allocation at fractional bitrates (EXL3 supports integer K per tensor; Q-Palette/fractional codes could add finer granularity); (4) quantising the 248k-vocab embedding/head so that bits saved there can be spent in the decoder.
- Note that the challenge's bytes-based size axis implicitly rewards formats with low metadata overhead (EXL3 trellis has essentially per-tensor/16x16-tile scale overhead vs K-quants' 4.5 bpw for "4-bit" and NVFP4's 4.5 bpw; GPTQ g128 ≈4.16 bpp per SLQ's map).

### Gaps
- No source gives an explicit rate-distortion number for TCQ at exactly 4 bits in the LLM-quantisation literature; the +0.08 dB (3-bit) figure is a secondary summary. The 2^{-8} bound and dB conversions above are standard formulae, not from a source.
- No published decomposition of residual KL into per-layer reconstruction vs propagation terms for a 4-bpw trellis quant; the closest quantitative statements are turboderp's Spearman-0.84 validation of additive per-tensor attribution and the 2609.01587 "damage is global" result.
